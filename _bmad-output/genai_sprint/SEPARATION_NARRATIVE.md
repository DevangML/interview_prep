# 1. THE CORE SCRIPT — "why are you available / why did you leave"

## 1.1 The rules, encoded

1. **Never lie if asked directly about a PIP.** Not a soft lie, not a technically-true evasion. TCS asks previous employers for reason-for-leaving on the BGV form. If you deny it in the room and ElasticRun's HR states it on a form, the offer dies — and it dies *after* you've been onboarded into a candidate pipeline, which is worse than not getting it.
2. **Do not volunteer it unprompted.** "My role at ElasticRun ended in «MONTH»" is a **complete answer**. It is true. It is not evasive. You are not obliged to narrate the internals of a separation any more than an employer is obliged to narrate why a requisition closed. Answer the question asked, at the altitude it was asked.
3. **Exactly one specific learning, named concretely.** Not "I learned a lot about myself." One named thing, one sentence, no follow-on. Contrition performed at length reads as either fragility or rehearsal.
4. **Never criticise ElasticRun, your manager, the process, or the fairness of it.** Not once, not in a joke, not in a "to be fair to them…" that carries a sting. The interviewer is scoring one thing here: *does this person become a problem when they're unhappy?*
5. **Three sentences, then stop.** Length signals shame. The silence after three sentences is the most persuasive part of the answer — let them fill it.
6. **Forward-looking close.** End on availability or on what you've built since, not on the exit.

## 1.2 ⭐ THE DEFAULT — use this unless probed

> "I'm at ElasticRun currently, serving a 30-day notice. Alongside that I've been building on the Gen AI side — I've got a permission-aware RAG system running end to end, with an evaluation harness, because that's the direction I want the next four years to go. Practically, I'm on a 30-day notice and my HR is aligned on releasing me at that."

Three sentences. **Then stop.** Do not add "so yeah", do not add "if you want I can explain more", do not add a nervous laugh. Hold the pause.

**Delivery notes.** Even tone, same pace as the rest of your answers. Do not drop your voice on "ended" — that's the tell that turns a fact into a confession. Eye contact through the first sentence. If your hands need something to do, keep them on the table, still.

## 1.3 The shorter variant — for HR, or when it comes up in passing

> "I'm serving a 30-day notice at ElasticRun. I've used that period building GenAI systems hands-on rather than coasting."

Two sentences. Use this when the question is administrative rather than probing — e.g. it appears inside a documents conversation, or as "so you're not working currently?"

## 1.4 The longer variant — only if they invite it

Use **only** if they explicitly ask you to expand ("tell me more about that", "what happened there?"). Never deploy this unprompted.

> "My role at ElasticRun ended in «MONTH». The honest version is that in my last stretch there, my manager and I weren't aligned on what good looked like in that role — I was pushing on architecture and tooling, and what the role needed more of at that moment was «THE TRUE THING — see §1.5». I'd own that; I read the priority wrong and I was slower than I should have been to check whether I was reading it wrong.
>
> What I've done with the time is deliberate rather than idle. I've built a permission-aware retrieval system end to end — a classification lattice plus role gating on every chunk, forty hand-written golden questions written as a spec before the system ran, and separate evaluation of retrieval, security and abstention behaviour. It's the thing I'd have wanted to be building anyway, and it's why I'm interviewing for a GenAI role rather than the role I had.
>
> And I'm available immediately, which I know matters when you're staffing against a project start date."

**⚠️ The «THE TRUE THING» placeholder is not optional and not for me to fill.** See §1.5.

## 1.5 The one named learning — choose the true one

You must name **one** concrete thing. It has to be true, because a PIP has documented feedback attached to it and a manager reference could contradict a fabricated one. Do not pick the one that sounds best; pick the one that was actually said to you.

Shapes that are honest *if they are true for you* — and which are recoverable rather than disqualifying:

- **Communication cadence.** "I under-communicated status upward. I was heads-down and assumed shipped work spoke for itself, and it doesn't — a manager who has to ask where something is has already lost confidence in it."
- **Priority alignment.** "I invested in the durable version of things when what was being asked for was the immediate version, and I didn't surface that trade explicitly enough to be overruled on it."
- **Measurement.** "I under-instrumented my own impact. I could describe what improved and I couldn't evidence it, and on a performance conversation that's the difference between a claim and a fact." *(This one is already consistent with your S4 story and your stated weakness in the HR file — it will cross-check cleanly.)*

**Disqualifying shapes — do not use, even if partly true:**
- Anything that relocates the cause to the manager, the team, the reorg, or the domain change.
- "I was bored / the work wasn't challenging." Reads as entitlement and predicts the same outcome at TCS.
- "There was no real feedback, it came out of nowhere." Even where true, it is unfalsifiable and it sounds like a person who does not hear feedback.

**Say it once. Do not return to it later in the conversation to add nuance.** Re-opening it is the single most common way candidates talk themselves out of a role they'd already survived.

# 2. THE ESCALATING PROBE LADDER

The structural rule: **each rung gets shorter and calmer than the one before.** The interviewer is testing composure under pressure, which is a real job requirement for a client-facing services role. A candidate who gets longer and more explanatory as pressure rises is signalling that the pressure is working.

---

**Rung 1 — "Why did the role end?"**

> "The role ended in «MONTH». My manager and I weren't aligned on priorities in the last stretch, and it was the right call to separate rather than stretch it out. I've used the time since to build on the GenAI side, which is where I wanted to be anyway."

*Three sentences. Neutral. Nothing to grab onto. If they move on — the answer worked; do not add to it.*

---

**Rung 2 — "Was there a performance issue?"**

> "Yes — in that role, in that last period, my manager's assessment was that I wasn't meeting expectations on «THE TRUE THING». I don't dispute the feedback. It's specific, and it's fixable."

*Do not hedge with "sort of" or "kind of". "Yes" costs you far less than a visible dodge. Two sentences plus a short third.*

---

**Rung 3 — "Were you on a PIP?"**

> "Yes. It didn't convert, and my role ended in «MONTH»."

*Two sentences. This is the moment. Answer flatly, without a change in tone, and stop. Do not explain the PIP process, do not describe its length, do not characterise it as unfair or as fair. The flatness is the answer — you are demonstrating that this is a fact you have processed rather than a wound you are carrying.*

**If they follow with silence:** hold it. Count to four in your head. Interviewers use the pause deliberately; filling it is where candidates volunteer the sentence that costs them the offer.

---

**Rung 4 — "What was the feedback specifically?"**

> "«THE TRUE THING», concretely. «One sentence of what that looked like in practice.» I read the priority wrong and I was slow to check that I'd read it wrong."

*Answer the actual question. Vagueness here reads as either dishonesty or as someone who never internalised the feedback — both worse than the feedback itself. Still three sentences.*

---

**Rung 5 — "Why should we believe that's fixed?"**

> "You shouldn't take my word for it — that's fair. What I'd point at is that the fix is a working habit, not an intention: «the concrete mechanism — e.g. I now write the spec and the acceptance check before I build, and status goes up weekly whether or not it's been asked for». You can test it in the first sixty days more cheaply than I can argue it."

*This is the strongest rung available to you. Conceding that belief must be earned rather than asserted is a seniority signal, and offering a short verification window is a confident move rather than a defensive one. Do not promise a probation period you'd resent — offer visibility, not conditions.*

---

**Rung 6 — "What have you done since?"**

*This is the rung you want to reach. Everything above was survival; this one is the argument. It is the only rung where you are allowed to run past three sentences.*

> "I built a permission-aware RAG system end to end — not a notebook, a real one. Sixteen-document enterprise corpus, and the interesting part is that authorisation is two-dimensional: a clearance lattice plus a role gate, both stored on the chunk at ingest, so the filter is a cheap boolean next to the vector rather than a post-hoc scrub of the answer. That design came directly out of the IAM work I did at ElasticRun — a role-only model forces you to over-grant or invent near-duplicate roles, and a clearance-only model lets a cleared user read a department they have no business in.
>
> I evaluate it as three separate systems, not one: retrieval — recall, precision, MRR, hit-rate; security — leakage rate and starvation rate; and behaviour — whether it abstains when it should. Leakage is the metric with a target of exactly zero, because recall at 0.85 is a good day and a leakage rate of 0.001 is a breach. Forty golden questions, hand-written from the corpus *before* the system ran, so the golden set is a specification rather than a recording of what the system happens to do.
>
> I've got the repo and the eval output with me if you want to look at it."

**Then offer the printout.** Do not just claim it. §6.

## 3.1 What TCS checks for lateral hires

| Check | What it involves |

## 3.2 Does "reason for leaving" and "rehire eligibility" get asked? Honestly:

**Yes, both are standard fields on Indian BGV employment-verification forms.** Reason for leaving is typically categorised as *voluntary / resignation / termination*, and rehire eligibility is a standard question on the verifier's template. ([factoHR — BGV process in India](https://factohr.com/background-verification-process/), [SutraHR — verifying past employment in India](https://www.sutrahr.com/how-to-verify-past-employment-in-india), [taggd — BGV glossary](https://taggd.in/hr-glossary/background-verification/))

**But what employers actually return varies enormously.** Practitioner reporting is consistent that many Indian employers answer these fields generically — "left on own volition", "better opportunity" — or decline to characterise performance at all, because saying more creates defamation exposure. Some answer literally. **You cannot know which ElasticRun does without asking.** That is exactly why §3.3 exists.

## 3.3 ⭐ WHAT TO FIND OUT FROM ELASTICRUN — TODAY

This is the highest-value hour available to you before Saturday. You are not asking for a favour or for anything to be softened. You are asking **what the record says**, so that what you say in the room matches it. That is a legitimate ask and HR handles it routinely.

**Call, don't only email.** Email creates the record; a call gets you the answer before Friday. Do both — call first, then send a short "confirming our call" email so it's on record.

### The questions, in priority order

1. **"What exact wording will my relieving letter carry?"** — Specifically: does it say *resigned*, *separated*, *services ended*, or *services terminated*? **Ask for a soft copy or a draft before Friday.** This is the single most consequential sentence in your file, because it determines which words you are allowed to use in the room.
2. **"Can I have the experience letter in the standard format — designation, date of joining, last working day?"** — A clean experience certificate with those three facts is what TCS most often accepts where a relieving letter is contested. Get it in writing on letterhead.
3. **"What is recorded in HRMS as my reason for separation, and what will ElasticRun state if a background-verification agency sends the standard employment-verification form?"** — Ask it exactly that way. You want to know the *form answer*, not the narrative.
4. **"Am I marked eligible for rehire?"** — Ask it flatly. If the answer is no, you are not being denied anything; you are learning a fact you need. It changes nothing about your script — you still never lie — but it tells you whether Rung 3 is likely to be reached via the paperwork rather than via your mouth.
5. **"Who handles BGV verification requests, and which email address should a verifier use?"** — Give TCS the correct route. A verification that bounces or goes to a dead mailbox stalls your onboarding for weeks and reads, wrongly, as evasion.
6. **"Is anything outstanding at my end — dues, assets, exit formalities — that could hold up my documents?"** — Close any of it today. A withheld document over an unreturned laptop is an avoidable, expensive delay.
7. **"Has my date of exit been marked in EPFO?"** — Only the employer can mark it. A blank DOE shows you as still employed at ElasticRun, which contradicts what you'll tell TCS. Ask for it to be updated.
8. **"Can I have the full & final settlement statement?"**

### If they refuse the relieving letter

State plainly what is true: a relieving letter is **not legally mandatory** under Indian labour law, though it is near-universal industry practice, and withholding it without valid cause can be escalated — internal HR escalation, then a formal notice, then the Labour Commissioner's office. ([taggd — is a relieving letter mandatory in India?](https://taggd.in/blogs/is-relieving-letter-mandatory-in-india/)) I have seen it claimed that state Shops & Establishments Acts (including Maharashtra's) mandate a *service certificate* on termination; **I could not verify that for Maharashtra specifically, so do not assert it to ElasticRun as a legal fact.** Ask a lawyer if it comes to that.

**The practical substitute stack**, which you should assemble regardless because it is entirely self-serve: appointment letter + experience letter + payslips + bank statements showing salary credits + Form 16 + EPFO service history. That stack has been accepted in place of a relieving letter in candidate reports — but that is HR discretion, not policy, so build it as insurance, not as a plan.

## 3.5 What actually happens if the PIP surfaces

Straight answer: **usually nothing, if you disclosed it.** The pattern in every candidate report that ends badly is the same — the candidate said one thing and the record said another. The PIP is not what kills offers. The *discrepancy* is.

Concretely:
- If you say "my role ended" and the record says "termination", there is no discrepancy. Those are compatible.
- If you say "I resigned" and the record says "termination", that is a discrepancy, and it is fatal. **Therefore: do not say you resigned unless your relieving letter says you resigned.** This is why §3.3 question 1 is question 1.
- If you are asked directly and answer honestly, and ElasticRun later confirms it, TCS's file shows a candidate whose account matched. That is a *positive* signal in a BGV file, not a neutral one.

Flag it to the TCS team if it's relevant."* That is the whole disclosure. Do not narrate the PIP to a recruiter over email — it becomes a forwarded artifact you don't control.

# 4. IMMEDIATE-JOINER LEVERAGE — real, but priced correctly

## 4.1 Why it is genuinely worth something

Indian service firms staff against **project start dates**, not against calendar-year headcount. A requisition exists because a client engagement has a date on it. A candidate who can be on the account in two weeks solves the recruiter's actual problem; a candidate who is 90 days out is a risk the account manager has to absorb.

The market data is unusually clear on this right now:

| Finding | Figure |
|---|---|
| Employer demand for immediate joiners, growth since 2022 | **+58%** |
| Candidate availability over the same period | **+12%** |
| Roles requiring joining within 15 days | **27%** — but only **14%** of candidates can meet it |
| Share of urgent hiring at mid-level (**3-6 years**) | **~40%** |
| Sectors leading urgent demand | IT/software and BFSI |

Source: foundit Employer Hiring Urgency Index, published 1 Apr 2026 — [The Hans India](https://www.thehansindia.com/hans/young-hans/immediate-joiners-emerge-as-employers-top-priority-survey-1061631), [Whalesbook summary](https://www.whalesbook.com/news/English/economy/India-Hiring-Demand-for-Immediate-Joiners-Jumps-58percent/69ca53e92850f02827f6f508). See also [Crescendo Global — why immediate-joiner roles are rising](https://www.crescendo-global.com/blog/2026/02/why-immediate-joiner-roles-are-increasing-across-indian-companies) (recruiter blog, **UNVERIFIED**).

That mid-level 3-6 year band is exactly the pool this requisition draws from — and it's the band with the *worst* immediate-availability supply. You are in the scarce quarter of a scarce pool.

## 4.2 What it buys you, honestly

**It buys:** faster process, priority in a shortlist, a real argument for grade flexibility, and standing to ask for a start date that suits you. It removes the single most common reason a TCS lateral offer dies — a candidate who accepts, then can't get released, then ghosts.

**It does not buy:** a large number. TCS lateral compensation is grade-banded and the recruiter negotiating with you usually cannot exceed the band regardless of how fast you can join. Treat availability as a **tiebreaker and a timeline asset**, not as a salary lever. Overplaying it ("I'm immediately available, so I should get more") reads as naïve about how a service firm's grade structure works — which is itself a negative signal for a services role.

## 4.3 The script

**Q: "What's your notice period?"**

> "Zero — I'm available immediately. I can join whenever your project date needs me, including this month if that helps."

*One sentence, then stop. Do not append "because I'm not working currently" — they will infer it, and volunteering it converts an asset into an explanation.*

**If they ask "so you're not currently working?":**

> "That's right — my role at ElasticRun ended in «MONTH», so there's no notice to serve. Practically it means no release risk on your side and no joining-date slip."

**If they ask you to commit to a date:**

> "Name the date and I'll hold it. The only thing I'd need is enough lead time for document verification on your side, and I've got my documentation set assembled already."

*Never promise a date you'd need luck to hit. You have no notice period, so almost any date is honest — which is precisely the advantage.*

**Where to deploy it as leverage, once and only once — in the compensation conversation:**

> "One thing worth putting on the table: I have no notice period. If the account needs someone on it this month rather than in the fourth quarter, I'm the version of this hire that doesn't slip. I'd ask you to weigh that alongside the number."

*Say it once, in that conversation, then let it sit. Repeating it makes it sound like the only thing you have.*

> "My last fixed at ElasticRun was «X» lakhs, and I can document that fully. For this role I'm looking in the range of «ASK LOW» to «ASK HIGH» fixed. I'd anchor that on two things rather than a market table — hands-on GenAI systems work rather than exposure to it, and breadth across three product domains and a full-stack surface. I'm flexible on structure. On timing I'm on a 30-day notice with HR already aligned, so I can move quickly against a project date."

**If they come in below your range:**

> "Understood. Can I ask what the constraint is — the grade I'd be mapped to, or the range for the role itself? If it's the grade, I'd rather discuss the level than trade the number down. And if fixed is capped, I'd want to look at the joining component and at when the first review lands."

**If they ask for a number before the technical round:**

> "Happy to — I'd just rather you'd seen the work first so the number reads properly. Broadly «ASK LOW» to «ASK HIGH» fixed, and I'm open on structure."

**Never say "as per company standards."** In Indian services hiring it reliably produces the bottom of the band. **Never invent a competing offer.** If you have one, state it factually with the number; if you don't, "I'm in early conversations elsewhere, nothing at offer stage" is true and sufficient.

## 5.4 Walk-away logic — the honest version

Most negotiation advice tells you to set a walk-away number. **Your walk-away is not a number right now, and you should not pretend it is.** Here is the actual calculus:

- You have **no income**, and the gap on your CV **grows every month** — and a growing gap is harder to explain than a recent one.
- You have **no competing offer**. A walk-away threat with nothing behind it is a bluff, and recruiters at a firm that hires at TCS's volume have seen every version of it.
- A TCS offer has **non-salary value that is real**: it closes the gap, it puts a large recognisable brand between you and the PIP for every subsequent BGV, and it resets your baseline for your *next* move — which is where you actually recover the money.

So, plainly:

- **Below ₹10 LPA fixed** *(and below your last drawn)*: this is the only genuine walk-away zone, and even then only if you have a second live process. A pay cut post-separation anchors your next move downward too, which compounds.
- **₹10–12 LPA**: accept if the grade and the project are right, and negotiate the *review timing* rather than the number — "when does the first review land, and what moves someone a band" is a more winnable conversation than the headline figure.
- **₹12 LPA+**: take it. Recover the delta on your next move in 18-24 months, from employed, which is the position where negotiation actually works.

**The one line to hold in your head:** the money you lose here is recoverable; another six months of gap is not. Negotiate properly, ask once, ask well — and do not blow up an offer over two lakhs from a position with no alternative. That is not weakness, it's arithmetic.

# 6. THE RECOVERY EVIDENCE — proof, not claim

## 6.1 What you actually built

`project/` is **PermRAG** — a permission-aware retrieval-augmented generation system. What makes it defensible as evidence rather than as a portfolio piece:

- **A real authorisation model, not a demo.** Two-dimensional: a four-level classification lattice (public / internal / confidential / restricted) *and* a role-intersection gate, both evaluated per chunk. Both dimensions are stored on the chunk at ingest, so the check is a cheap boolean over metadata already sitting next to the vectors — not a post-hoc scrub of the generated answer.
- **A traceable lineage to your real work.** The design rationale is written in the source: a role-only model forces over-granting or near-duplicate role proliferation; a clearance-only model lets a cleared user read a department they have no business in. **That is the exact lesson from your IAM permission-matrix work at ElasticRun.** The project is a continuation of your professional experience, not a pivot away from it.
- **Evaluation as three systems, not one.** Retrieval (recall@k, precision@k, MRR, hit@k), security (leakage rate, starvation rate), behaviour (abstention precision/recall). Leakage carries a target of exactly zero — recall@5 of 0.85 is a good day, a leakage rate of 0.001 is a breach.
- **A golden set that is a specification.** 40 hand-written questions in `data/golden.jsonl`, written from the corpus *before* the system ran, with per-question expected documents, the querying principal, and whether abstention is the correct behaviour.
- **Config-driven ablation.** Everything tunable lives in `config.py` so an ablation is a config change, not a code change — the same instinct as the Campaigns engine.

## 6.2 How to use it as proof rather than as a claim

**The difference is that a claim is spoken and proof is handed over.**

Bring, printed and stapled, in a plain folder:
1. **One page:** the authorisation predicate and the design rationale (the docstring at the top of `permrag/principals.py` is already written as the argument — print it as-is).
2. **One page:** the evaluation framing from `permrag/evaluate.py` — the three-system split and the leakage-target-zero line.
3. **One page:** your actual eval output. Real numbers from a real run. **Run it before Friday and print whatever it says, including the parts that aren't flattering** — a printed metric that shows a weakness you can explain is worth ten times a rounded claim.
4. **Ten lines of `golden.jsonl`**, so they can see the spec is real.

Then, in the room: **"I've got it with me — want to look at it?"** and put it on the table. Do not deliver a monologue about it first. An interviewer who opens the folder has stopped interviewing your gap and started reviewing your work, which is the entire objective.

## 6.3 The framing rules

- **Say it's yours, built outside employment.** "This one's mine — built in the last few weeks, not client work." Interviewers respect the clean disclosure far more than a vague implication, and it makes everything else you say more believable.
- **Do not call it production.** It isn't. Say "it runs end to end offline on a sixteen-document corpus" — precise beats impressive.
- **Do not quote numbers you haven't run.** Every number you speak must come off your printed eval output.
- **Frame the time as invested, because it was.** Not "I've been job hunting." Not "I've been keeping busy." The true sentence is: *"I decided that if I was going to have a gap, it should produce something."*
- **Do not use the project to dodge the PIP question.** If you pivot to the project at Rung 2, it reads as evasion and they will push harder. Answer the rung, *then* the project is the natural landing at Rung 6.

## 6.4 The one sentence that ties the whole file together

> "The gap is real and it's about «N» weeks. I decided that if I was going to have one, it should produce something — so it produced a permission-aware retrieval system with an evaluation harness, and that's the work I'd like to be judged on."
