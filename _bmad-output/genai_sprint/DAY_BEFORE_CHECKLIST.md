## 1.5 · THE OPENING PITCH — read it once, then put it away

Say it aloud **once**, standing, timed. Not five times — you have said it a dozen times this week
and over-rehearsing tonight makes it sound recited rather than known.

**The four beats:** *who and how much → the AI angle → the stack → why this role.*
**Landmarks:** pause half a beat after *"six hundred tracked items."* End on *"that's a systems
problem more than a model problem"* and **stop** — it invites exactly the follow-up you want.

# PART 3 — IN THE BUILDING

## 3.1 · The mechanics
- **Hand over a CV copy unprompted** when you sit down.
- **Ask for the interviewer's name** if it isn't offered, and use it once.
- **Sit up, feet flat, hands visible.** Offline interviews are judged on physical presence in a way
  video ones aren't.
- **Take notes when they talk** — especially in MR. It signals you treat their words as information
  rather than as a break before you speak again.
- **Do not fill silence.** After you deliver a Result, **stop.** Indian panels frequently pause to
  see whether you'll ramble. Let it sit.
- **Never contradict a number across rounds.** Experience, CTC, notice, joining date. Say the same
  thing three times.

## 3.2 · Close every round the same way
> "Thank you — is there anything about my background you'd like me to go deeper on before we
> finish?"

This surfaces an unspoken objection while you can still answer it. If the experience-band concern
is sitting unsaid in that person's head, this is the question that lets it out — and you have a
prepared answer for it.

## 3.3 · If a round goes badly
Rounds may collapse — MR and HR are commonly merged for laterals. If someone starts asking about
notice period and salary inside what you thought was the managerial round, **that is normal and
usually a good sign.** A long wait between rounds usually means you passed. No offer on the day is
normal and is **not** a rejection signal — the pipeline runs a week or more.

## 3.4 · Afterwards, the same evening
- [ ] Message your Covenant contact: how it went, which rounds you cleared, confirm your interest.
- [ ] **Write down every question you were asked, that night.** Memory decays fast and this is the
      single most valuable artifact for any subsequent round or any other company.

# THE FIVE THINGS THAT ACTUALLY LOSE THIS DAY

Not knowledge. These:

1. **Wrong campus.** Confirmed in writing? Screenshotted? Map pin saved?
2. **A missing document** — most commonly payslips or the degree certificate. Over-pack the folder.
3. **Inconsistent numbers** across TR, MR and HR.
4. **Getting defensive when the experience band comes up.** You have a prepared, confident answer.
   Deliver it **once**, well, and move on. Repeating it signals *you* think it's disqualifying.
5. **Running out of energy in round three.** Eat. Hydrate. Don't burn yourself out being brilliant
   in the waiting area.
