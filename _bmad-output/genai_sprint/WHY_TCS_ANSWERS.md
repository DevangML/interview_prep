# 🎤 "WHY TCS · WHY THIS BU · WHAT EXCITES YOU"

**The problem:** every candidate gets asked these, so the average answer is worthless. Interviewers have heard *"TCS is a global leader with a rich legacy"* two hundred times and it registers as noise.

**The fix:** every answer below contains at least one thing **only you could say.** That's what makes it sound like a person instead of a prepared statement.

---

## 🚫 THE FIVE THINGS THAT MAKE AN ANSWER SOUND ENGINEERED

1. **Adjectives instead of specifics.** "Cutting-edge, innovative, dynamic" = nothing. A number, a project name, or a moment = something.
2. **Too long.** Rehearsed answers run 90 seconds. Real ones run 30-40. **If you're still talking at a minute, you're performing.**
3. **No texture.** Real answers contain something slightly unflattering, or unexpected, or admitted. Perfectly flattering answers read as constructed.
4. **Corporate vocabulary.** *passionate · leverage · synergy · journey · ecosystem · align.* Nobody talks like this. Don't start on Saturday.
5. **Flattering the company.** They know where they work. Praise reads as filler. **Talk about the work, not the brand.**

---

# ① "WHY DO YOU WANT TO JOIN TCS?"

> "Honestly, the pull was the business unit before the company.
>
> I've been at ElasticRun three years — genuinely good place to learn, I shipped across three different product domains there. But the clients are Indian retail and distribution, and the problems have a ceiling on them. Nobody's going to ask me to prove a system is correct to a regulator.
>
> What TCS has that I can't get where I am is the client base. If I want to build GenAI systems where being wrong actually costs something, I need to be near clients where being wrong actually costs something. That's a services firm with enterprise BFSI clients, and TCS is the largest of those.
>
> I'd rather say that plainly than tell you I've always dreamed of working here."

**Why it works:** the last line is the texture — mildly disarming, obviously true, and it buys credibility for everything before it. *"The problems have a ceiling on them"* is a real observation about his own job, not a compliment to theirs.

**⏱ ~35 seconds. Then stop.**

---

# ② "WHY THIS BU — BFSI?" ⭐ *your strongest answer*

> "This one's almost a coincidence in my favour.
>
> Before I knew the BU was BFSI, I'd already built a permission-aware RAG system — retrieval where the access-control filter is applied *before* the vector search, so a user can only ever retrieve what they're cleared to see.
>
> I built it because I'd spent a stretch on IAM at ElasticRun — RBAC, field-level permissions across five personas, default-deny approval workflows. So when I started reading properly about RAG, the first thing I noticed was that none of the tutorials handle permissions at all. Every example assumes one flat corpus and one user. That bothered me enough that I built the version that doesn't.
>
> Then I saw BFSI-America on the invite. And that's the domain where that problem isn't optional — a relationship manager and a compliance officer asking the same question should get different answers. In most industries that's a nice-to-have. In banking it's the requirement."

**Why it works:** it's a **story with a chronology**, not a claim. He built it *before* knowing — which cannot be reverse-engineered to flatter them, and is verifiable in the repo. The observation *"every example assumes one flat corpus and one user"* is the kind of thing you only say if you actually read the tutorials.

**⏱ ~45 seconds.** Slightly longer is fine here — it's the answer carrying the most weight.

---

# ③ "WHAT EXCITES YOU?"

> "The gap between a demo that works and a system you'd actually defend.
>
> When I built that retrieval system, I could have stopped fairly early — it was returning good answers within a day. What I actually spent most of the time on was evaluation: forty questions, twelve of which the system is *supposed* to refuse, and measuring whether it refused the right ones.
>
> Then I ran ablations to find out which of my design choices were actually load-bearing. Two of them weren't. I had MMR turned on because everyone turns MMR on — and when I measured it, it cut precision almost in half. So I turned it off.
>
> That's the part I find genuinely interesting. Not getting something to work — finding out which parts of it were doing nothing. And in a regulated domain that stops being a preference and becomes the job."

**Why it works:** it answers with **evidence of a habit**, not an enthusiasm. Admitting *"I had MMR on because everyone turns MMR on"* is the texture — a small, honest, slightly unflattering detail that no constructed answer would include. And it lands on the BFSI point without reaching for it.

**⏱ ~40 seconds.**

## 🎣 THE FOLLOW-UPS

**"You could get enterprise clients at Infosys or Wipro too — why TCS?"**
> "Fair. Honestly the BU is what I'm answering, not the logo — this is the GenAI role in BFSI that came to me, and it's the work I want. If you're asking whether I'd have said yes to the same role elsewhere: probably yes. I'd rather be straight about that than pretend otherwise."

*Never bluff brand loyalty. Interviewers can hear it, and honesty here costs nothing.*

**"Services is very different from product — you'll be on client timelines, not your own."**
> "I know, and I've thought about whether that would frustrate me. What I keep coming back to is that at ElasticRun I mostly built things nobody outside the company used. Client work has constraints, but the constraints are what make the problem real. I'd rather have hard requirements than total freedom over something low-stakes."

**"What do you know about our BFSI practice?"**
> *Answer from `BFSI_DOMAIN.md` — the WisdomNext platform and the Everest PEAK Matrix Leader placement. Then pivot back to the work, fast. Don't perform research.*

---

## 🎯 DELIVERY

- **Say them at conversation pace, not presentation pace.** These three are the moments to sound most like yourself.
- **Let one sentence be slightly imperfect.** A small restart or a "honestly" is what a real answer sounds like. Flawless delivery on these reads as recited.
- **Stop early.** The silence after 35 seconds is confidence. Filling it is not.
- **Never say:** passionate · leverage · journey · ecosystem · cutting-edge · synergy · "always wanted to work for TCS."

**Practise them twice. Not ten times.** These are the three answers that get *worse* with over-rehearsal — you want them thought-through, not memorised.
