## 2. Theoretical Foundation 1: Christensen's Disruption Theory in Technical Hiring

### 2.1 Low-End & New-Market Disruption in Recruitment

In Clayton Christensen’s framework, incumbents focus on sustaining innovations—improving performance along metrics traditional customers historically value (e.g., speed of writing syntax manually, memorization of obscure algorithms). Big Tech interviewing processes act as sustaining incumbent filters.

However, the tech industry is undergoing a structural paradigm shift driven by AI. Hiring managers are caught in a **Capability Trap**: they test for manual coding speed (sustaining metric), but their actual operational bottleneck is architectural design, AI context engineering, agentic workflow control, and system reliability.

```
  Performance
```

* **Low-End Disruption**: Devang undercuts traditional candidates on prep time by utilizing the **80/20 Pareto Rule** for hard skills (mastering 20 core patterns in 30 days rather than 500 problems), ignoring low-yield algorithmic edge cases.
* **New-Market Disruption**: Devang targets hiring managers who need to build AI-native systems, agentic workflows, and high-velocity engineering organizations—a market segment traditional LeetCode candidates are unqualified to serve.

3. **Autonomous Execution**: "Take ambiguous business goals and independently orchestrate end-to-end solutions."

By positioning Devang explicitly as the candidate who solves these three JTBDs better than any conventional candidate, we shift the evaluation criteria entirely in his favor.
