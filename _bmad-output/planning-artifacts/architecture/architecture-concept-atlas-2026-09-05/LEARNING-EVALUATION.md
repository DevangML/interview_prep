# Concept Atlas learning evaluation

Status: planned; engagement and camera depth are not learning evidence.

## Outcome model

For each concept, measure four separable outcomes: prediction of an unseen case, explanation of the mechanism and cost, diagnosis of a counterexample, and transfer to an unfamiliar language or architecture. Capture immediate and delayed checks with content and rubric versions.

## Pilot design

Use the same concept set and allocate learners to the spatial reader, accessible text reader, and retrieval-enhanced reader. Keep instruction time and source material comparable. Randomize concept order where prerequisites permit, pretest baseline knowledge, collect immediate post-test and delayed test, and record hints/reveals. Treat this as a feasibility pilot, not a causal claim about all learners.

## Rubric

Score each response for problem identification, mechanism accuracy, enforcement boundary, explicit trade-off, counterexample handling and transfer. A visited node, completed animation or self-rating never marks mastery. Preserve raw responses and rubric versions for audit; local-only attempts are self-assessments until a reviewer or validated grader is available.

Retrieval tasks are included because a controlled Science study reported stronger conceptual learning for retrieval practice than its concept-mapping condition; that finding motivates the comparison but does not establish this atlas's effect. [Karpicke and Blunt](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1199327).

## Decision rule

Do not claim the atlas improves acquisition until the preregistered pilot reaches its minimum sample, delayed-transfer results are available, attrition is reported, and the same rubric is applied across conditions. If outcomes are mixed, retain the spatial view where it improves orientation and use retrieval prompts where they improve recall.
